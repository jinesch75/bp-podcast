# Podcast Script — "Zesumme Vereinfachen – Helping to Simplify Public Services Together"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 9 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** And today we have a topic that, honestly, almost everyone has feelings about: paperwork.
**ANNA:** Yes! Forms, procedures, administrative steps. Sometimes they are simple. And sometimes, they are not.
**TOM:** We have all had that moment. A form that asks the same thing twice. A step that makes you think: why is this so complicated?
**ANNA:** Well, today's topic is a project that turns that frustration into something useful.
**TOM:** It is called Zesumme Vereinfachen. The name is in Luxembourgish.
**ANNA:** And it means "simplifying together". I love that idea — doing it together.
**TOM:** So Anna, what exactly is this project?
**ANNA:** It is a participation platform where citizens and businesses can help make the State's procedures, and administrative steps in general, simpler.
**TOM:** So instead of just complaining about paperwork, you can actually do something about it.
**ANNA:** Exactly. Its slogan says it beautifully: public services that make your daily life easier.
**TOM:** And who is behind it?
**ANNA:** It is run by the Ministry for Digitalisation, through a body called the Simplification Council.
**TOM:** The platform was launched in 2022, and it is part of a bigger effort to simplify the administration.
**ANNA:** That bigger effort even has its own name: "Einfach Lëtzebuerg", which means "Simply Luxembourg".
**TOM:** With a Ministry for Digitalisation behind it, I would expect it to be all about apps and websites.
**ANNA:** Many people think that, but it goes much wider. It is about administrative steps in general: forms, offices, counters, and how close public services are to the people who use them.
**TOM:** Let's get to the heart of it. How does the platform actually work?
**ANNA:** The big idea is to put the real experience of citizens and businesses at the centre.
**TOM:** Because the people who use a procedure every day often understand its problems best.
**ANNA:** So citizens and businesses can take the initiative, and submit ideas to simplify the State's procedures.
**TOM:** You have noticed something clumsy, something that could be easier? You can suggest how to fix it.
**ANNA:** And then the work happens in a participatory, co-creative way, together with the people who proposed it. Ministries and administrations can also post their own projects there, to ask for people's opinions.
**TOM:** I think it helps to picture it as a project that moves through phases.
**ANNA:** Yes. There is a phase for sharing ideas, and then phases for working on a solution and testing it.
**TOM:** And here is a detail I really appreciate. You don't have to commit to the whole journey.
**ANNA:** That's right. You can take part in just one phase, or several, as much as you want.
**TOM:** So you are free. You give what you can, when you can. No pressure.
**ANNA:** And the platform welcomes everyone, in all four of our languages: Luxembourgish, German, French, and English.
**TOM:** That really matters in a country as multilingual as Luxembourg. Everyone can join in their own language.
**ANNA:** And what about people who do not use the internet much? Can they take part too?
**TOM:** Yes, they can, because in some projects people could also give their opinion on paper, in workshops, at stands, or at round tables. We will hear two real examples in a moment.
**ANNA:** Let's make this concrete. What kind of thing could be simplified?
**TOM:** Imagine a form that asks for information the State already has. Why fill it in again?
**ANNA:** Or a procedure that involves three different offices, when one would do.
**TOM:** Or instructions that are hard to understand, that could be written in plain language.
**ANNA:** Those are good ideas. But has the platform already changed something real?
**TOM:** It has, and my favourite example is about meeting the administration face to face. For a long time, if you wanted help with a procedure in person, you went to the Guichet.lu reception desk in Luxembourg City.
**ANNA:** That can be a long trip for some people, depending on where they live.
**TOM:** Exactly. The government had promised more reception desks in the regions. So the Ministry for Digitalisation asked people what they would need from a reception desk close to home.
**ANNA:** And how did they ask people: only online, or in other ways too?
**TOM:** Not only online, and that is the interesting part. People could answer on the platform, on a paper form, in workshops, and at stands.
**ANNA:** So even people who rarely go online could give their opinion.
**TOM:** That's right. In the end, nearly eight thousand people took part.
**ANNA:** That is a lot of voices. And what did all those people tell the State?
**TOM:** The results clearly showed one need: public services should be closer to people, especially to those who find it hard to do their procedures online.
**ANNA:** And what came out of it in the end?
**TOM:** A bus! It is called Guichet.lu on tour. It is a fully electric bus, fitted out like a reception desk, and it travels to the different regions of the country.
**ANNA:** So the counter comes to you. And what can you do on board?
**TOM:** You can get help with a procedure, or with using MyGuichet.lu. You can find the right service for your question, or apply for a Luxembourg identity card or driving licence.
**ANNA:** I really like that. People's ideas did not lead to a new app, but to a real bus, with real people to help you.
**TOM:** Is there also an example where a ministry asks for people's opinion on a bigger plan?
**ANNA:** Yes, and it is about water. Every six years, Luxembourg updates its plan for managing its water, as a European directive requires.
**TOM:** And what is in a plan like that, exactly?
**ANNA:** It describes the state of our waters, the main issues, and the measures planned to protect and restore them.
**TOM:** And who prepares this plan, and with whom?
**ANNA:** The Water Management Administration is preparing the fourth plan, for 2027 to 2033. It is preparing it together with citizens, communes, businesses and experts.
**TOM:** And how can ordinary people join in?
**ANNA:** Through public consultations, and through round tables, seminars and workshops in different places. You can follow all of it on the platform.
**TOM:** So simplifying together is not only about forms. It is also about shaping public decisions together, online, on paper, or around a table.
**ANNA:** Let's talk about why this approach matters, beyond the paperwork itself.
**TOM:** First, it saves time. Simpler procedures mean less stress, for citizens and for staff.
**ANNA:** Second, it builds trust. When the State listens, people feel respected, and feel part of things.
**TOM:** And third, it is democratic, in a quiet, everyday way.
**ANNA:** Yes. Democracy is not only voting every few years. It is also helping to shape the services you use.
**TOM:** And that fits the spirit of the Biergerpakt perfectly: citizens and the State, working together.
**ANNA:** There is also something hopeful here. Frustration can be turned into improvement.
**TOM:** Instead of "this is annoying", it becomes "here is how we could make it better".
**ANNA:** So Tom, let's make it practical. How can someone actually take part?
**TOM:** First, next time a procedure frustrates you, write the idea down instead of just sighing.
**ANNA:** Second, visit the platform and share your suggestion in your own language, or join a workshop or a round table when a project offers one.
**TOM:** Third, if you like, follow it through one or more phases, and help co-create the solution.
**ANNA:** And fourth, remember: even a small idea can make a real difference for many people.
**TOM:** Let's bring it all together. What should people remember about the platform?
**ANNA:** First, it is a platform where citizens and businesses help simplify the State's procedures, online and offline.
**TOM:** Second, it is run by the Ministry for Digitalisation, and launched in 2022, as part of "Einfach Lëtzebuerg".
**ANNA:** Third, you can submit ideas, join one phase or many, in any of the four languages.
**TOM:** And fourth, it has already helped change real services, like the Guichet.lu bus that brings the counter closer to people.
**ANNA:** Paperwork may never be exciting. But the chance to make it simpler, together, is genuinely empowering.
**TOM:** So the next time something feels too complicated, remember: your voice can help fix it.
**ANNA:** That was our episode about this platform for simpler public services.
