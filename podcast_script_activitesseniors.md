# Podcast Script — "Who Does What for Seniors? – Organisations Worth Knowing"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 11 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** I have a question to start with, Anna. If your mother or your grandfather needed some support, would you know which organisation to call?
**ANNA:** To be honest, not always. There are many names, and it is not easy to know who does what.
**TOM:** That is exactly today's topic. We are going to present organisations in Luxembourg that work for older people and for their families.
**ANNA:** And the good news is that all of them are listed together in one place, on a website called Info-Seniors.
**TOM:** The address is infosenior dot public dot l-u. It is the public register of services for older people, and it is run by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees.
**ANNA:** We already made a full episode about this website. You can find it in our list of episodes, under the name Info-Seniors.
**TOM:** So what are we going to do differently in today's episode?
**ANNA:** Today we open one section of that website. It is called "Senior activities", and at the time of this recording it lists ten services.
**TOM:** The name "Senior activities" makes me think of gymnastics and card games. Is that what this section is about?
**ANNA:** Not only. In this register, the term is much wider. It covers services that inform, advise, train and accompany older people and the people close to them.
**TOM:** And what do these ten services have in common?
**ANNA:** Each one has an official approval from the ministry. Its page in the register shows its contact details and its documents, so everything is transparent.
**TOM:** Ten names are a lot to remember. How shall we go through them?
**ANNA:** We will sort them into five small groups: staying active and learning, feeling less alone, memory and dementia, help for family carers, and the end of life.
**TOM:** Let's start with staying active and learning. Which organisation comes first?
**ANNA:** The first one is GERO, the competence centre for old age. It is based in Luxembourg City.
**TOM:** And what does GERO do for people in everyday life?
**ANNA:** GERO informs and trains people on everything about ageing. The idea is that we can live this stage of life actively and with a calm mind.
**TOM:** I have heard that GERO has several departments.
**ANNA:** Yes, it has three. The first is called geroAKTIV. It is for people aged sixty and over, with activities, talks, seminars and courses, so that learning never stops.
**TOM:** And what do the other two departments do?
**ANNA:** The second one, geroPRO, offers practical training to professionals who work with older people. The third one, geroRESEARCH, does applied research on ageing.
**TOM:** So GERO works for seniors, for professionals, and for knowledge. Which organisation is next in this group?
**ANNA:** Next is the Generatiounstreff of Amiperas, in Luxembourg City, in the district of Bonnevoie. Amiperas is an association for people who are retired, older or living alone.
**TOM:** Generatiounstreff means a meeting place for the generations. So what happens in this meeting place?
**ANNA:** Three times a year, it publishes a programme of activities. There are creative workshops, cooking, gentle sport and walks, culture and music, day trips and journeys.
**TOM:** Is it only a place for leisure, or is there more?
**ANNA:** There is more. You can also get information and advice there, for example about retirement, about digital tools or about administrative steps. And there are projects between generations and between cultures.
**TOM:** That sounds like a friendly address. And what is the third name in this group?
**ANNA:** The third one is GoldenMe. This association helps older people to feel at home in the digital world.
**TOM:** So that means computers, smartphones and the internet.
**ANNA:** Yes. GoldenMe offers information, courses and events for people who did not grow up with these tools, so that they gain basic digital skills.
**TOM:** I like that, because so many services are online today.
**ANNA:** And GoldenMe gives special attention to generations working together. Younger and older people learn from each other.
**TOM:** There is one more name in this first group, and I find it wonderful: Super Senior.
**ANNA:** Super Senior is a service of the association MEC, which is based in Echternach. And here, the seniors are the ones who help.
**TOM:** Whom do the seniors help, and how does it work?
**ANNA:** The association looks for seniors who help children in primary school with their homework. It is school support between generations.
**TOM:** So a retired person shares time and patience, and a child gains confidence.
**ANNA:** Exactly, and both sides win. Now let's move to the second group: feeling less alone.
**TOM:** Loneliness can touch anyone, and very often nobody sees it.
**ANNA:** That is why the Luxembourg Red Cross has a service called Iris. It accompanies every person who is touched by loneliness.
**TOM:** And how does the Iris service do that in practice?
**ANNA:** Iris trains volunteers. These volunteers then offer their presence regularly to a person who feels isolated. They come back again and again, and a real link can grow.
**TOM:** Is the Iris service only for older people?
**ANNA:** No. Iris makes no difference of age, culture or social background. And it has been doing this work for many years.
**TOM:** So someone who has a little free time could also become a volunteer there.
**ANNA:** Yes, the volunteers are the heart of this service, and Iris prepares them well for their role.
**TOM:** Our third group is about memory and dementia. Two services are listed here.
**ANNA:** The first one is the Info-Zenter Demenz. It is the national centre for information and consultation on all questions about memory problems and dementia.
**TOM:** And who can contact the Info-Zenter Demenz?
**ANNA:** Everyone can: people who have memory problems or dementia, their relatives, health professionals, and anyone who is simply interested. And all the consultations and documents are free.
**TOM:** We also have a full episode about the Info-Zenter Demenz, for those who want to know more.
**ANNA:** The second service has a French name, "Accompagnement et Conseil", which means support and advice. It belongs to the Association Luxembourg Alzheimer.
**TOM:** What is the difference between this service and the information centre?
**ANNA:** This service goes one step further into daily life. It gives practical advice about dementia, and it puts families in contact with the right services.
**TOM:** Families often become very tired when they care for someone with dementia.
**ANNA:** Yes, and the service explains to relatives how they can get some relief. Together with them, it builds a solution that fits their needs.
**TOM:** Does it also help families with the paperwork?
**ANNA:** It does. It helps with administrative steps, and it organises and coordinates the help that is needed within the long-term care insurance.
**TOM:** That brings us to the fourth group: the people who care for a family member at home.
**ANNA:** Many older people are supported by a partner, a daughter, a son or a neighbour. These carers do a lot, and they need support too.
**TOM:** Which service in the register is there for them?
**ANNA:** It is called the Berodungsdëngscht, which means advice service. It belongs to the foundation Stëftung Hëllef Doheem, which provides help and care at home.
**TOM:** And what does this advice service offer?
**ANNA:** It offers training courses for carers, information and consultations. Its team of specialists can also give psychological support, and advice from occupational therapists, physiotherapists and dietitians.
**TOM:** What is the goal behind all this work?
**ANNA:** The goal is that a person can stay at home as long as possible, with as much independence as possible. And the family is supported socially, physically and emotionally.
**TOM:** Now we come to the last group. It is a more serious subject: serious illness, the end of life, and grief.
**ANNA:** It is not easy to talk about, but it belongs to life. And two organisations in the register are there for these moments.
**TOM:** The first one is Omega 90. I think many people in Luxembourg have heard this name.
**ANNA:** Omega 90 is a non-profit association, created in 1990 and recognised as being of public utility. It accompanies people with a serious illness, people at the end of their life, their families, and people who are grieving.
**TOM:** What does Omega 90 do in practice?
**ANNA:** It runs Haus Omega, a centre for palliative care. There, the aim is to ease suffering, so that people have the best possible quality of life in the last phase of their life.
**TOM:** And what is there for people who are not staying in Haus Omega?
**ANNA:** Omega 90 offers psychological and social consultations, for patients, for their relatives, and for people in mourning. It also trains professionals in palliative care.
**TOM:** Are volunteers involved here as well?
**ANNA:** Yes. Omega 90 leads a network of volunteers who accompany people at the end of life. It trains them and supports them during all their time as volunteers.
**TOM:** And what is the second organisation in this group?
**ANNA:** It is called Mäi Wëllen, Mäi Wee. In English, that means: my will, my way. It is the association for the right to die in dignity, and it is also recognised as being of public utility.
**TOM:** What is the role of this association?
**ANNA:** It informs the public about the Luxembourg law of 2009 on euthanasia and assisted suicide. And it reminds us how important it is to talk about our wishes for the end of life with our family and with our family doctor.
**TOM:** So it is first of all about information, and about talking in good time.
**ANNA:** Yes. The association also keeps in contact with doctors, who sometimes have questions on this subject. And it accompanies each person in their own reflection and their own decisions.
**TOM:** Thank you, Anna. That was ten services. How can our listeners find them again?
**ANNA:** Go to infosenior dot public dot l-u and choose "Senior activities". The website exists in French and in German. Right next to it, there is also a section for the Clubs Aktiv Plus, the activity clubs all around the country.
**TOM:** And what do we see for each service?
**ANNA:** You see a short description, the address, the phone number, the email address and the website. There is also a map, and documents such as the service brochure or the annual report.
**TOM:** Do I need to be a senior myself to contact one of these services?
**ANNA:** No, that is not necessary at all. Many of them are also there for families, for carers, for volunteers and for professionals. If you are not sure, simply call and ask.
**TOM:** And I imagine that this list can change over time.
**ANNA:** Yes, services can be added and details are updated, so the website is always the best place to check.
**TOM:** Let's bring it together. What should people remember?
**ANNA:** First: to stay active, to learn and to meet people, there are GERO, the Generatiounstreff of Amiperas, GoldenMe for the digital world, and Super Senior, where seniors help children.
**TOM:** Second: against loneliness, there is Iris, the service of the Red Cross with its volunteers.
**ANNA:** Third: for memory problems and dementia, there are the Info-Zenter Demenz and the support and advice service of the Association Luxembourg Alzheimer.
**TOM:** Fourth: family carers find training and advice at the Berodungsdëngscht of Stëftung Hëllef Doheem.
**ANNA:** And fifth: around serious illness, the end of life and grief, there are Omega 90 and Mäi Wëllen, Mäi Wee.
**TOM:** And all of them are together in one place, on infosenior dot public dot l-u.
**ANNA:** Nobody has to go through these moments alone. That was our episode about the organisations that work for seniors in Luxembourg. This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt, a programme that favours living together in Luxembourg: discovering the country, and meeting new people. You simply need to join the Biergerpakt for free at biergerpakt.lu.
**TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care, and see you next time!
**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
