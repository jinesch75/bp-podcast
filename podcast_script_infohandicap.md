# Podcast Script — "Info-Handicap – Nothing About Us Without Us"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 11 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** Have you ever noticed someone wearing a strap around the neck with little yellow sunflowers on it, in a shop, at the airport or on the train? Many people see it without knowing what it means.
**ANNA:** It is a quiet signal: the sunflower tells others that the person has a disability that you cannot see, and that they may need a little more time, patience or help. In Luxembourg, you can get this sunflower from Info-Handicap, and Info-Handicap is our topic today.
**TOM:** Let's start at the beginning, then: what exactly is Info-Handicap, and who is behind it?
**ANNA:** Info-Handicap is the National Council for Persons with Disabilities in Luxembourg. It is a non-profit association, and it has worked for the rights of people with disabilities, in Luxembourg and at international level, since 1993. Today it brings together more than sixty member organisations.
**TOM:** And what kind of organisations are these members? A few examples would help us to picture them.
**ANNA:** They all work in the field of disability. Some bring together people with a certain condition, such as autism, Down syndrome, multiple sclerosis or Parkinson's disease. Others are there for people who are blind or hard of hearing, or for wheelchair users. And there are sports organisations, such as Special Olympics Luxembourg, and services that offer housing and care.
**TOM:** That is a lot of experience in one place. The members elect the board of Info-Handicap, which takes the important decisions, and a team puts these decisions into practice. And the whole organisation follows one motto: "Nothing about us without us."
**ANNA:** That is a strong motto: what does it mean in practice?
**TOM:** It means that decisions about people with disabilities should never be taken without them. The motto comes from the European Disability Forum, where Info-Handicap represents Luxembourg. And people with disabilities take an active part in the work of Info-Handicap itself.
**ANNA:** Why does a country need a council like this? Are there really so many people concerned?
**TOM:** More than many people think: according to the census of 2021, about one person in seven in Luxembourg lives with a disability. The most common are visual, motor and hearing impairments, and many disabilities cannot be seen at all. Info-Handicap also points out that people with disabilities are more often unemployed than others.
**ANNA:** So the work is about rights, but also about everyday life, and Info-Handicap works in four big areas: information and advice, accessibility, training, and raising awareness. Let's go through them one by one.
**TOM:** Let's begin with information and advice: who can turn to Info-Handicap with a question?
**ANNA:** Everyone can: people with or without a disability, their families and friends, employers, people who work with people with disabilities, and anyone who is simply interested. Its information and advice service is the contact point for every question about disability.
**TOM:** What kind of questions do people bring to this service most often?
**ANNA:** Very often, the questions are about work, about school and education, and about accessibility. In some cases, Info-Handicap can also arrange legal advice. And it has the right to join a court case as a civil party, which means that it can take part in the case itself.
**TOM:** That is good to know, and how can listeners get in touch with the information and advice service?
**ANNA:** You can call 366 466, or write an email to info@iha.lu. A meeting with an adviser is arranged by appointment, and the office is in Luxembourg City, open from Monday to Friday.
**TOM:** Before they call, is there something that people can read at home to find their way?
**ANNA:** Yes: Info-Handicap publishes a disability guide in six parts, which you can download from its website. They cover early childhood, school and education, work and income, special measures and financial aid, transport and mobility, and finally rights and legal support.
**TOM:** The second big area is accessibility. Accessibility means that everyone, with or without a disability, has the same chance to take part in daily life: in buildings, on the internet, in transport, in tourism, in culture and in communication. Luxembourg has laws on accessibility, and our episode about accessibility explains them.
**ANNA:** And what does Info-Handicap do in practice to make places more accessible?
**TOM:** It informs and advises, for example on easy-to-read language and on sign language. It trains future technical accessibility inspectors, the people who check whether a place follows the rules, and during that training they also meet people with disabilities and hear about their needs. And it promotes a quality label for accessible tourism, called EureWelcome.
**ANNA:** Who is this label for: only for people with disabilities?
**TOM:** No, it is not: it follows the idea of design for all. The label recognises places and events that work for everyone: people with disabilities, but also older people, families, and people who can only move with difficulty for a while, for example after an accident. You find more on eurewelcome dot l-u.
**ANNA:** Another part of accessibility is about getting around. Info-Handicap runs a service called SAMT, which helps people with disabilities learn to use public transport on their own and safely.
**TOM:** How does that work in practice? Does somebody travel with you?
**ANNA:** Yes: it starts with a first talk about your goals, what you can already do, and what support you still need. Then you get a personal learning plan, and you practise the route step by step with a trainer, with maps or pictures to help you find your way.
**TOM:** And what happens if something goes wrong on the way: a bus that does not come, or a stop that has moved?
**ANNA:** That is part of the training too: you prepare for the unexpected, such as a cancelled bus, a late train, a changed stop, or feeling unwell. And you choose what you want to learn: all public transport, only one route, or only one means of transport, such as the tram.
**TOM:** For people who cannot use public transport at all because of a severe and lasting disability, there is also Adapto, a special transport service. Info-Handicap's website explains who can use Adapto and how to book trips with its app.
**ANNA:** Let's move on to the third area, training. Who are these courses for?
**TOM:** Mostly for companies, public services and associations. Info-Handicap is recognised by the Ministry of Education as a training centre. It offers awareness half-days, courses on meeting and talking with people with disabilities, a course that prepares a team to welcome a new colleague with a disability, and courses made to measure.
**ANNA:** There are also short workshops for staff, of about two hours, in small groups. They can be held in Luxembourgish, French, German or English, and people with disabilities take part to share their experience.
**TOM:** Let's come back to the sunflower from the beginning of this episode: Info-Handicap is the Luxembourg member of the international Hidden Disabilities Sunflower network. The sunflower symbol is on a strap that you wear around your neck, with a card that you can fill in yourself. On the card, you can note what you need, for example more time or help to find your way, without having to explain your disability in detail.
**ANNA:** And where can I get one, and do I need a medical certificate or some other kind of proof?
**TOM:** No, you do not need any of that: Info-Handicap gives the strap and the card free of charge to anyone who asks, without registration and without proof. You simply go to its office in Luxembourg City, on a weekday, in the morning or in the afternoon.
**ANNA:** The fourth area is raising awareness. All year round, Info-Handicap runs campaigns so that people understand disability better. It also coordinates MOSAIK, a network of social and cultural organisations for inclusive culture: artists with and without disabilities on the same stage, and shows with audio description, subtitles or sign language.
**TOM:** And Info-Handicap also speaks for people with disabilities to the ministries and the political parties. Luxembourg has committed to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, and Info-Handicap follows closely the national action plans that put this convention into practice.
**ANNA:** I have read that Info-Handicap also works on elections: what does it do there?
**TOM:** Yes, it wants elections that are accessible to everyone. Since a change in the law in 2023, people under full guardianship can vote as well. Info-Handicap holds workshops on how voting works, how to get to the polling station and which aids exist. And this podcast has an episode about voting in your commune, called I Can Vote.
**ANNA:** Before we finish, the website is info-handicap dot l-u, in German and in French. What are the main points to keep in mind from this episode?
**TOM:** First, Info-Handicap answers every question about disability, for people with or without a disability, their families and their employers: you can call 366 466. Second, it works for accessibility, from training inspectors to helping people learn to travel by bus, train and tram on their own.
**ANNA:** And third, if you have a disability that cannot be seen, you can get a sunflower strap and card free of charge at its office. Because a society works better for everyone when nothing about us is decided without us. That was our episode about Info-Handicap, the National Council for Persons with Disabilities. This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt, a programme that favours living together in Luxembourg: discovering the country, and meeting new people. If you want to discover more activities offered by the Biergerpakt, such as guided visits, online information sessions, a language learning mobile application, creative workshops or training sessions, simply head over to biergerpakt.lu where you will find detailed information. It is open to all residents, luxembourgish and non-luxembourgish, as well as to cross-border workers. And the activities are free of charge. You simply need to join the Biergerpakt for free at biergerpakt.lu.
**TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care, and see you next time!
**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
