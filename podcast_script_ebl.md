# Podcast Script — "EBL – Environmental Advice for Luxembourg's Communes"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 8 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** And today we meet an organisation that works quietly in the background... but you have probably seen its work in your own commune.
**ANNA:** Maybe a colourful poster in the town hall. Or a page about saving water in your commune's magazine.
**TOM:** There's a good chance it came from the EBL.
**ANNA:** EBL stands for Ëmweltberodung Lëtzebuerg. In English, that means "Environmental Advice Luxembourg".
**TOM:** "Ëmwelt" is the Luxembourgish word for environment, and "Berodung" means advice.
**ANNA:** So Tom, what exactly is the EBL?
**TOM:** It's a non-profit association, created in 1992. It's neutral and independent, and it gives advice on protecting the environment.
**ANNA:** And who does it advise?
**TOM:** Mainly the communes. But also associations, public and private institutions, businesses, and environmental advisers.
**ANNA:** Most of Luxembourg's communes are members. Together, they are home to the vast majority of the people living in the country.
**TOM:** So even if you've never heard the name, the EBL probably works with your commune.
**ANNA:** How is it financed?
**TOM:** Through the membership fees of its members, and through an agreement with the Ministry of the Environment, Climate and Biodiversity.
**ANNA:** Why do communes need this kind of help?
**TOM:** Because a lot of environmental work happens locally. Waste, water, green spaces, energy in public buildings, events in the village...
**ANNA:** And a small commune can't have an expert for every topic.
**TOM:** Exactly. The EBL is a network. Communes share ideas and experience, and they don't have to reinvent the wheel each time.
**ANNA:** It's also a meeting point for the environmental advisers who work in the communes.
**TOM:** And if a commune wants to create its own environmental service, the EBL can help... from defining the tasks to recruiting an environmental adviser.
**ANNA:** Let's talk about the Climate Pact and the Nature Pact.
**TOM:** These are agreements between the State and the communes. The communes commit to concrete measures for the climate and for nature.
**ANNA:** And the EBL supports the communes in these pacts, with training, practical exchanges and ideas.
**TOM:** Now, let's look at what the EBL actually does. There's a lot!
**ANNA:** First, training. The EBL organises seminars, workshops and conferences, often together with partners.
**TOM:** For example, conferences on more circular construction... how to build, and take buildings apart, in a way that wastes fewer resources.
**ANNA:** Second, publications. The EBL is famous for its infographics.
**TOM:** These are simple, colourful pages that explain one topic. The value of an old tree. Using rainwater in the garden. Saving energy with the right heating.
**ANNA:** Many are available in several languages, and communes can print them in their magazine or share them online.
**TOM:** Third, material to borrow. There's an energy measuring kit, a device that finds the hidden electricity eaters in your home, and a CO2 meter for the air in a room.
**ANNA:** And there are teaching kits too, like one about the living soil, and one about our rivers and lakes.
**TOM:** Fourth, campaigns. The EBL coordinates several national campaigns.
**ANNA:** One of the best known is "Ouni Pestiziden"... that means "without pesticides".
**TOM:** It's supported by around twenty Luxembourg partners. It shows that green spaces and gardens can be beautiful without chemical pesticides.
**ANNA:** Since 2016, communes in Luxembourg are no longer allowed to use pesticides on public spaces. And many private gardeners want to do the same.
**TOM:** The website ounipestiziden dot l-u has tips and good examples for your own garden.
**ANNA:** Another project is Green Events. Luxembourg has so many village parties, festivals and sports events.
**TOM:** Green Events helps organisers make their event more sustainable. Less waste, local products, reusable cups, better mobility...
**ANNA:** It was launched by the environment ministry, and it's coordinated by the EBL together with the SuperDrecksKëscht.
**TOM:** And the most sustainable events can get the Green Events label. So if you help organise a party for your club, have a look at greenevents dot l-u.
**ANNA:** I also love the idea of Gutt Geschier. "Geschier" means tools.
**TOM:** It's a library of tools. Instead of buying a drill that you use twice a year, you can borrow one, for free.
**ANNA:** It's open to everyone, residents and associations. You'll find the borrowing points on guttgeschier dot l-u.
**TOM:** There's even a project for greener websites, called Grénge Web, and one that gives unused school material a second life.
**ANNA:** And for children, there's a lot too.
**TOM:** The EBL offers workshops for school classes. In one of them, children follow the journey of a T-shirt and learn about plastic in the oceans.
**ANNA:** And a new one takes classes to a farm, to learn about healthy food, soil and biodiversity.
**TOM:** The EBL has also published children's books in Luxembourgish, for example about zero waste, and about pollinating insects like bees.
**ANNA:** So teachers and childcare services can find good material here.
**TOM:** Let's make it practical. How can you, as a resident, benefit from the EBL?
**ANNA:** First, look out for the EBL infographics in your commune's magazine. They are full of small, easy steps.
**TOM:** Second, if you need a tool, try Gutt Geschier before you buy.
**ANNA:** Third, if your association organises an event, think about the Green Events label.
**TOM:** Fourth, if you're a teacher, look at the workshops and teaching kits.
**ANNA:** And fifth, ask your commune about its environmental service or its environment commission. Many communes welcome residents who want to get involved.
**TOM:** That's a lovely way to meet your neighbours, by the way. Very Biergerpakt!
**ANNA:** And if you're looking for a job in the environment, the EBL also shares green job offers with its network.
**TOM:** You can follow the EBL's news through its newsletter, which comes out a few times a year, or on social media.
**ANNA:** The website is e-b-l dot l-u. It's mostly in Luxembourgish, with some material in French, German and English.
**TOM:** And you can write to info at e-b-l dot l-u.
**ANNA:** Let's bring it together. What should people remember about the EBL?
**TOM:** First... it's a neutral, non-profit association that gives environmental advice, mainly to the communes.
**ANNA:** Second... most communes are members, so its work reaches almost everyone in Luxembourg.
**TOM:** Third... it offers training, infographics, material to borrow and workshops for schools.
**ANNA:** And fourth... it runs campaigns and projects like Ouni Pestiziden, Green Events and Gutt Geschier, which everyone can use.
**TOM:** Protecting the environment often starts with small steps, close to home.
**ANNA:** And it's easier when we take them together, in our commune. That was our episode about the EBL, the Ëmweltberodung Lëtzebuerg. **TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care... and see you next time!
**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
