# Podcast Script — "SIMPA – Information and Mediation for Older People"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 10 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** Many families know this moment. Someone at the table says that Mum is finding the stairs difficult, or that Grandpa might need some help at home, and suddenly there are a lot of questions and nobody really knows where to start.
**ANNA:** And very often, those questions come with worries: which service is the right one, what are our rights, and what do we do if something does not go well later? So today, we talk about a public service that was created for exactly these moments. It is called SIMPA.
**TOM:** SIMPA is the national information and mediation service for older people in Luxembourg. It deals with the services for older people, such as care at home or a place in a residential facility. The letters come from its French name, but everybody simply says SIMPA.
**ANNA:** Who runs this service, and how long has it existed?
**TOM:** It is a public service of the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. It was created by the law of 23 August 2023 on the quality of services for older people, and it was presented to the public in March 2025.
**ANNA:** What does a law about quality change for an older person or for a family?
**TOM:** When SIMPA presents its work, it sums up the law in four ideas: more information, more transparency, more quality, and more say for the people who use the services.
**ANNA:** SIMPA helps to make those ideas real for all the services for older people that are approved by the ministry. First, the places where people live: residential facilities, with staff around the clock, and assisted living, where you have your own flat with help close by, and then the day centres.
**TOM:** And for people who want to stay in their own home, are those services covered too?
**ANNA:** Yes, they are: home help and home care, meals on wheels, and the telealarm, which sends help in an emergency, day and night. And also the senior activities and the Club Aktiv Plus, which has its own episode in this podcast.
**TOM:** Who exactly can turn to SIMPA, and how much does it cost?
**ANNA:** The older person who uses one of these services, their family and the people close to them, and even the organisations that run the services. Carers, professionals and anyone who is interested are welcome too. And it costs nothing: all the services of SIMPA are free of charge.
**TOM:** SIMPA describes its work with three simple ideas: listening, understanding and explaining, and strengthening the dialogue. Explaining means clear information about the services, and about the rights and duties of residents, users and organisations, because knowing them prevents many misunderstandings.
**ANNA:** And for listening, SIMPA has a telephone line that is older than the service itself: the Seniorentelefon, which has existed since 1999. You can call it with any question or worry linked to ageing, or simply because you need someone to listen. The number is 247-86000.
**TOM:** Who answers when you call, and who is the line meant for?
**ANNA:** A member of the SIMPA team answers, in Luxembourgish, French, German, Portuguese or English. The line is open in the morning, from Monday to Friday, for older people, their families and carers, and professionals. And when a question belongs somewhere else, the team tells you where to turn.
**TOM:** The third idea is dialogue: SIMPA wants good communication between residents or users and the organisations, so that a small problem can be solved before it becomes a real conflict.
**ANNA:** And if there is a disagreement anyway, what should you do first?
**TOM:** First, speak directly with the organisation that provides the service. Every organisation must have a way to handle complaints, and its contact details are in the service's establishment plan, which you can read on infosenior dot l-u. That website has its own episode in this podcast.
**ANNA:** And what happens when the direct conversation does not work, or becomes too difficult?
**TOM:** Then SIMPA can offer a mediation, if everyone involved agrees. The people in a disagreement try, of their own free will, to reach an agreement, with the help of a mediator who is independent, impartial and competent.
**ANNA:** What kind of disagreement can be brought to a mediation, and who can ask for it?
**TOM:** It must be about a service for older people: for example the care and help, the meals, hygiene, the living situation, or the relationship between a family and a service. The person who uses the service, the people close to them, or the staff of the organisation can ask for it, by phone, by e-mail or with the contact form on the website.
**ANNA:** And the person who leads the mediation, whose side is she on?
**TOM:** She is on everyone's side. The website even calls her "omni-partial", which means that she is there for all the people at the same time. She has no power to decide: she helps the people to find their own agreement, and what is said stays confidential.
**ANNA:** Can a son or a daughter ask SIMPA to speak on behalf of their mother in a mediation?
**TOM:** That is not possible, because SIMPA does not represent anyone. The older person stays at the centre: they must agree to take part, and the mediation respects their independence, their dignity and their needs, and makes sure that they are fully heard.
**ANNA:** The steps are well described on the website. It often starts with individual talks: the mediator meets each side separately, explains the rules, and checks whether a mediation makes sense. Then everyone signs a written agreement to try a mediation.
**TOM:** Where do the meetings take place, knowing that not everyone can travel easily?
**ANNA:** Mostly in the meeting room of the service, in Luxembourg City. But a meeting can also take place in the residential facility, or an appointment can be arranged outside the office. You can also bring a relative, a legal adviser or an expert, if you announce it in advance.
**TOM:** And how does a mediation end, when it goes well?
**ANNA:** It ends with an agreement, complete or partial. The mediator can suggest writing it down, with the date, everyone's signature and the concrete commitments of each side.
**TOM:** The website gives an example that makes all this very concrete. Mr Muller is eighty-two and lives in a residential facility, and he and his daughter Sophie disagree about his social activities and about the way he is cared for.
**ANNA:** And what is the aim of the mediation in a case like that?
**TOM:** The aim is to find a balance between three things: Mr Muller's independence, the professional framework of the carers, and the worries of his daughter.
**ANNA:** I like that it is not about who is right, but about a solution that everyone can live with. Are there situations where SIMPA is not the right address?
**TOM:** There are three such situations. First, administrative decisions, for example how much a social security body pays: there, you can turn to the Ombudsman or to the social courts. Second, questions about health care, which belong to the health mediator. Third, conflicts at work between members of staff.
**ANNA:** The Ombudsman and the health mediator both have their own episodes in this podcast. Many families also have questions about money, and SIMPA's website gives an overview of the public help, for example the long-term care insurance, a supplement called COMPA, or help with housing.
**TOM:** Will the team also fill in the application forms for you?
**ANNA:** That is not part of its work: SIMPA does not handle the paperwork itself, but it tells you which service is responsible. And to see all the approved services in one place, there is the public register on infosenior dot l-u.
**TOM:** How can people reach SIMPA, if they have a question or would like an appointment?
**ANNA:** By phone, by e-mail, or with the contact form on the website, and you can also meet the team in person, by appointment. The website is simpa dot l-u, and it exists in French and in German.
**TOM:** When SIMPA presents itself, it ends with three things to remember. Shall we go through them together?
**ANNA:** First: who do you call? The Seniorentelefon, on 247-86000, for information, for advice, or simply for someone who listens.
**TOM:** Second: SIMPA listens, shows you the right way, and offers a mediation when there is a disagreement with a service. It is neutral, confidential and free of charge.
**ANNA:** Third: you find all the approved services at a glance on infosenior dot l-u.
**TOM:** And one more piece of advice from us: if something does not go well, talk about it early. A good conversation often solves more than a long quarrel.
**ANNA:** Because getting older should never mean being left alone with your questions. That was our episode about SIMPA, the information and mediation service for older people. This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt, a programme that favours living together in Luxembourg: discovering the country, and meeting new people. If you want to discover more activities offered by the Biergerpakt, such as guided visits, online information sessions, a language learning mobile application, creative workshops or training sessions, simply head over to biergerpakt.lu where you will find detailed information. It is open to all residents, luxembourgish and non-luxembourgish, as well as to cross-border workers. And the activities are free of charge. You simply need to join the Biergerpakt for free at biergerpakt.lu.
**TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care, and see you next time!
**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
