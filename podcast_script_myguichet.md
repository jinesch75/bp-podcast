# Podcast Script — "MyGuichet.lu: Your Online Door to Luxembourg's Administrations"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 9 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.

**TOM:** Hello everyone!

**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.

**TOM:** Exactly. And today we talk about something very useful. Especially if you just arrived in Luxembourg. It's called MyGuichet.lu.

**ANNA:** Right. So, Tom... let's start at the beginning. What is MyGuichet.lu?

**TOM:** Okay, so... Guichet.lu is the State's information website, and MyGuichet.lu is its secure online space. Together they give you quick, user-friendly access to all the information, procedures and services offered by Luxembourg's public administrations and bodies. MyGuichet.lu is secure, and it's your... let's say, your single point of contact with the public administrations. So instead of going to an office, you can do many administrative procedures from home. On your computer, or on your phone.

**ANNA:** So it's like... an online counter?

**TOM:** Exactly. Open every day, at any hour.

**ANNA:** Nice. And, hmm... what can I actually do with it?

**TOM:** Quite a lot, really. The main thing is... you can do administrative procedures online.

**ANNA:** Without printing anything?

**TOM:** For many procedures, yes. No printing, no envelope, no stamp. And there's more. On MyGuichet, you have your own personal space where you can follow the status of your requests... so you can see, okay, my file arrived, it is being processed, and so on. And in your eSpace you can also see the personal data the State has about you. The official data — for example from the national registry. You can also receive official messages and documents there, electronically. And... one more thing... you can book appointments online with some administrations.

**ANNA:** Okay, wait, let me repeat that. So I can... do procedures online, follow my files, check my official data, receive documents, and book appointments.

**TOM:** That's it. And you can also store important documents in your eSpace, so they are always with you. Oh — and there is a mobile app, too, for your smartphone.

**ANNA:** Very complete. Now... the big question. Why should I use it? I mean, I can still go to the office, right?

**TOM:** Of course, you can. But the offices are open during the day, when many of us are at work. MyGuichet is open twenty-four hours, seven days a week. You save time — no queue, no waiting. And everything stays in one place.

**ANNA:** And it's in English?

**TOM:** The website, yes — Guichet.lu exists in French, German and English. And many procedures too. Not all of them, but many. So for someone who just arrived and doesn't speak French yet... it really helps.

**ANNA:** Mmm, that's important. Okay. So now, how do I start? What do I need?

**TOM:** Right, so... to register on MyGuichet, you need a few things. First, you must be at least sixteen years old. Then you need your Luxembourg national identification number. That's a thirteen-digit number — people in Luxembourg call it the "matricule". You get it when you register in Luxembourg, or when you start working here.

**ANNA:** The matricule, okay. Cross-border workers have one too, right?

**TOM:** Yes, if you work in Luxembourg, you have one. It's on your social security card, for example. Then you need an email address, and a computer — for the first registration, a laptop or desktop, not your phone. And one more thing: a way to prove your identity online.

**ANNA:** Ah, and this is where LuxTrust comes in?

**TOM:** Exactly. LuxTrust is a company that provides secure digital identity. The most popular product is LuxTrust Mobile — an app on your phone. When you log in somewhere, the app asks you to confirm. Many people already use it for their online banking.

**ANNA:** Okay, so the question many listeners will have... do I need LuxTrust to use MyGuichet?

**TOM:** Good question. The answer is... not necessarily. You need one secure way to log in, but you have a choice. It can be a LuxTrust product, yes. Or the Luxembourg electronic identity card if you have a Luxembourg ID card with the certificates activated. Or, and this is interesting for people from other European countries... an eIDAS login from your own country.

**ANNA:** eIDAS... that's the European system, right? So I could use, for example, my Belgian or German electronic ID?

**TOM:** Exactly. If your country has one of these national systems, you can often use it to log in to MyGuichet — you still need your matricule. But with a foreign login, you can't sign some forms, like your tax return. And one more thing — some simple procedures work even without logging in at all. But then you have fewer options. You can't save the form and continue later, and you can't follow the status. So... having a real account is much better.

**TOM:** But honestly, if you live or work here, LuxTrust Mobile is the easy way. You install the app once, and... that's it. And if you already use it for your bank, you can use the same one.

**ANNA:** Good. So, let's say I have my matricule and my LuxTrust. What do I do?

**TOM:** It's quite simple. You go to Guichet.lu, you click on "Log in", and you follow the steps with your email and your matricule. Then you create your personal eSpace. It takes a few minutes. And there are tutorials on the website, step by step.

**ANNA:** Perfect. Now... what kind of procedures are we talking about? Which areas?

**TOM:** Almost everything in daily life, really. Let's see... citizenship — so identity card, passport, criminal record. And here is a nice one: as a foreign resident, you can apply to register on the electoral rolls — so you can vote in the communal elections. And if you're a citizen of another EU country, in the European elections too. Then immigration — residence permits, for example. Very important when you arrive. Then family and education... taxation — yes, you can do your tax return online. Transport — your driving licence, registering a car. Health and social security. Housing. Financial aid. Work and pension... and even leisure, like registration at the National Library.

**ANNA:** Wow. So many areas are covered, all in one place.

**TOM:** That's right. And maybe one word for the entrepreneurs listening: businesses can use MyGuichet too, with a business eSpace, for things like tax and VAT declarations, social security declarations for their staff, or applying for permits and licences.

**ANNA:** Good to know. Okay, Tom, let's wrap up. Short summary?

**TOM:** Sure. MyGuichet.lu is the secure online platform of the Luxembourg State. You can do your administrative procedures from home, follow your files, receive official documents, and book appointments. To use it fully, you register with your matricule and a secure login.

**ANNA:** And if you need help, many tutorials are available, and a helpdesk.

**TOM:** Really, it makes life in Luxembourg easier. Especially in the first months.

**ANNA:** That was our episode about MyGuichet.lu. This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt, a programme that favours living together in Luxembourg: discovering the country, and meeting new people. If you want to discover more activities offered by the Biergerpakt, such as guided visits, online information sessions, a language learning mobile application, creative workshops or training sessions, simply head over to biergerpakt.lu where you will find detailed information. It is open to all residents, luxembourgish and non-luxembourgish, as well as to cross-border workers. And the activities are free of charge. You simply need to join the Biergerpakt for free at biergerpakt.lu.

**TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care... and see you next time!

**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
