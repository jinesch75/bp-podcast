T: You get on the bus, you sit down, and nobody asks you for a ticket. For many visitors, that is a big surprise, but in Luxembourg it is completely normal.
A: And that is our topic today: public transport in Luxembourg, and mobiliteit.lu, the website and the free app that help you find your way. So let me start with the obvious question: is public transport really free, and for whom?
T: Yes, it really is. Since March 2020, public transport has been free in the whole country, and Luxembourg was the first country in the world to do this for all its buses, trains and trams. It is free for everyone: for residents, for cross-border workers, and for tourists.
A: And it covers a lot. There are the trains of the CFL, the national railway company. There is the national bus network, called RGTR, with more than two hundred lines between towns and villages. There are city buses, for example in Luxembourg City and in the south of the country. And there is the tram in Luxembourg City, which now goes all the way to the airport.
T: So I can simply get on any bus or tram, but surely there is something I need to have with me?
A: You do not need a ticket, but the official rules say that you must carry a valid identity card. You also have to respect the rules of the transport companies. And one detail for families: children under four must travel with someone who is at least twelve years old.
T: That is easy to remember. But I suppose that free travel also has a few limits: where do I still have to pay?
A: There are two of them, and both are easy to understand: first class on the train, and any journey that goes beyond the border. Let's start with first class: how does it work?
T: First class on the train is still paid, for everyone from the age of twelve. You can buy a ticket for a short trip, a ticket for the whole day, or a subscription by the month or by the year if you travel often. The prices are listed on mobiliteit.lu.
A: And where do I buy that ticket? I remember that there used to be a special travel card for it.
T: That is right, but that card is no longer valid. Since the start of 2026, you buy your ticket in the CFL go app, on the CFL website, or at a ticket machine in the station. You can no longer buy one from the bus driver. And if you need help, the staff at the big CFL sales centres can show you how the app or the machines work.
A: Now to the second limit, the border. Many people come to work here from France, Belgium and Germany every day, so this concerns a lot of our listeners. Free travel stops at the border, so for the part of the trip abroad, you need a cross-border ticket.
T: And on the train, do I have to pay for the whole journey, or only for a part of it?
A: Only for the part abroad. Inside Luxembourg, second class stays free up to the border. And there is a small bonus: a few stations just across the border, in Belgium and in France, are treated like Luxembourg stations, so the trip to and from them is free as well. The list is on mobiliteit.lu.
T: And what about the buses that cross the border: are the rules the same there?
A: They are very similar. The bus is free up to the last stop before the border, and to go further, you need a ticket for the cross-border zone, called RegioZone. Since 2026, there is one single RegioZone fare for the bus links with the neighbouring countries, with one exception: an express coach line has its own price.
T: And one practical point: the ticket is checked at the latest at the last stop in Luxembourg before the border, so buy it before you set off. You buy it in the same places as a first class ticket: in the CFL apps, on the website, or at a ticket machine. We talk more about life across the borders in our episode about the House of the Greater Region.
A: So far, we have talked about vehicles and tickets. Now let's talk about finding your way, and that is where mobiliteit.lu comes in. Who is behind it?
T: Mobiliteit.lu is a service of the Public Transport Administration, which is part of the Ministry of Mobility and Public Works. This administration plans the national bus network and the school buses, it runs a call centre, and it brings all the travel information together, on the website and in the app.
A: I often use the website at home. You type where you start and where you want to go, and it shows you the best connections, with all the buses, trains and trams together. You also find the timetables of every line, maps of the networks, and a page about disruptions and roadworks, which is very useful before a trip.
T: The app is free on iPhone and on Android, but what does it add compared to the website?
A: Real time, above all. In the app, you see the next departures at the stop where you are, with the delays as they happen. You can look up a line and follow its route on a map. And you can save your favourite trips with a star, so that you find them again in one tap.
T: That sounds practical, and can the app also warn me when my bus is late, before I leave the house?
A: Yes, it can: you set a personal alert for a trip, and the app sends you a message on your phone. There is also a special section for school buses, which parents and pupils find handy. And if you have an idea to improve the app, there is a button to send your feedback.
T: The part I like most is called My Mix. You choose the ways you like to travel, for example the bus, the train, the bike, the car or walking, and the app suggests trips that combine them. For each option, it can show you things like the travel time, the distance and the impact on the environment.
A: Speaking of bikes, can I take mine on the train, and does the app help people who cycle too?
T: Yes to both questions. Inside Luxembourg, your bike travels free on the train, as long as there is enough space, and on CFL trains, the places for bikes are behind the doors marked in green. The app also shows the bike-sharing stations near you, how many bikes are available, and routes for cycling. And it shows charging points for electric cars, and car parks where you can leave your car and continue by bus, train or tram.
A: And what else am I allowed to bring on board? I am thinking of a pram, a dog or a big suitcase.
T: You can travel with luggage, prams, wheelchairs, bicycles, pets and assistance dogs, such as guide dogs. The rule is simple: you are responsible for them, they must be safe and secured, and they must not block the corridor, the seats or the other passengers.
A: And for an evening out, there are night buses. Many communes run them at weekends, and Luxembourg City has its own night buses as well. They are free like the rest, and you find their timetables on mobiliteit.lu, in the section called Nightbus. It is a safe way to get home without taking the car after a party.
T: Public transport should work for everyone, including people who find it very hard to get around. For people with a serious disability who cannot use the normal buses and trains, the Public Transport Administration organises a special service called Adapto.
A: How does Adapto work, and who is allowed to use it?
T: It is for people with a lasting disability that strongly reduces their mobility, and you need an Adapto card, which you can request through Guichet.lu. You book each trip in advance, by phone through the mobiliteit.lu call centre, online or in the app. The vehicle picks you up at the kerb in front of your starting point, and the service is only for trips inside Luxembourg.
A: The same administration also organises Mobibus, a regular special transport, mainly for pupils with special needs who travel to their school or to a specialised centre. And in our episode about Info-Handicap, we presented a service that helps people with disabilities learn to use ordinary public transport on their own.
T: Those are important services. And if I have a question, or if something went wrong on my trip, who do I contact?
A: You can call the mobiliteit.lu call centre on 2465 2465. It is open every day, also at weekends and on public holidays. You can also write to them through the contact form on the website, with your questions, your suggestions or your complaints.
T: And behind the scenes, the same administration runs a control centre that keeps an eye on the national bus network while the buses are running. It also deals with the complaints that travellers send in, so your message reaches the people who organise the buses.
A: Let me add one safety tip for Luxembourg City and the area of the airport: the tram tracks are reserved for the tram. Look carefully before you cross them, and always follow the signs and the traffic lights near the tracks.
T: Let's bring it all together. First, buses, trams and trains in second class are free in the whole country, and you only pay for first class and for the part of a trip abroad. Second, you buy those tickets in the CFL apps, on the CFL website or at a ticket machine. Third, mobiliteit.lu and its free app plan your trip in real time, with every way of travelling.
