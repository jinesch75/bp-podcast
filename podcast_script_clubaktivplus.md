# Podcast Script — "Club Aktiv Plus – Stay Active, Meet People"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 9 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** I have a question to start with, Anna. What would you do with your time if you no longer had to go to work every morning?
**ANNA:** I think I would enjoy the first few weeks. But after that, I would probably miss my colleagues, and I would want something to do and people to see.
**TOM:** Many people feel exactly that when retirement comes closer. And that brings us to today's topic: the Clubs Aktiv Plus, and their website.
**ANNA:** The address is clubaktivplus dot l-u, written in one word.
**TOM:** Let's begin with the basics. What exactly is a Club Aktiv Plus?
**ANNA:** It is a meeting place in your region. People go there for leisure, for learning, to meet others, and also to get advice.
**TOM:** And who are these clubs made for?
**ANNA:** They are for everyone who wants to stay active when retirement is coming closer, or when it has already begun.
**TOM:** Is there a minimum age to take part?
**ANNA:** No, there is no age limit. The clubs are mainly aimed at people around retirement age, but other age groups are welcome too.
**TOM:** The name sounds quite new to me. Have these clubs existed for a long time?
**ANNA:** The first ones were created in 1999. For many years, people simply called them the senior clubs.
**TOM:** And why did the name change?
**ANNA:** The new name arrived in 2024. Many members are very active, and they did not really recognise themselves in the word "senior". Club Aktiv Plus fits them much better.
**TOM:** Who is behind these clubs?
**ANNA:** It is an initiative of the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees — the same ministry that organizes the Biergerpakt.
**TOM:** Does the ministry run every club itself?
**ANNA:** No. It works as a partnership. Communes join forces to create a club, and an organisation manages it. The ministry approves the club and helps to pay for it, together with the communes.
**TOM:** And how many of these clubs are there in the country?
**ANNA:** There are more than twenty clubs, from the north to the south of Luxembourg. Together, they work with more than eighty partner communes.
**TOM:** What if my own commune is not a partner of any club?
**ANNA:** That is not a problem at all. Every club welcomes you, wherever you live.
**TOM:** So I could even go to two or three different clubs.
**ANNA:** Yes, you can choose the clubs and the activities you like, as long as there are free places.
**TOM:** Do I have to become a member first, with a card and a yearly fee?
**ANNA:** Some clubs have a membership card, but most of them don't. And a card is never required to take part in an activity.
**TOM:** Now let's talk about the fun part. What can people actually do in these clubs?
**ANNA:** The website sorts the activities into four big families. The first one is health and well-being.
**TOM:** I imagine that means sport and movement.
**ANNA:** Yes: yoga, dance, cycling, Nordic walking, water gymnastics, and gentle exercises for the back. But also memory training, relaxation, and cooking classes.
**TOM:** That is already a long list. What is the second family?
**ANNA:** The second one is about good company and getting involved. Think of shared lunches, board games, a quiz afternoon, singing in a choir, or a game of skittles.
**TOM:** And what do we find in the third family?
**ANNA:** The third one is discovery and culture: cinema, theatre, concerts, museums, hikes, and day trips in Luxembourg and abroad.
**TOM:** That leaves the fourth family of activities.
**ANNA:** The fourth one is learning and staying informed. There are language courses, computer and smartphone courses, and talks about current topics.
**TOM:** Computer courses are useful, because so many things happen online today.
**ANNA:** Exactly, and some courses even explain online banking. There are creative workshops too, such as photography, drawing, sewing, or arranging flowers.
**TOM:** So it is clearly more than an afternoon of card games.
**ANNA:** Much more. The people who run the clubs describe them as lively places of exchange and lifelong learning.
**TOM:** Who leads all these activities?
**ANNA:** It depends on the activity: the staff of the club, specialised trainers, or volunteers.
**TOM:** Let's make it practical. Do I have to pay to take part?
**ANNA:** Most activities have a price, because there are materials, transport, entrance tickets or a trainer to pay. But every club also offers some activities that are free.
**TOM:** And do I need to sign up before I come?
**ANNA:** Usually yes. Registration guarantees your place, and the club can tell you if something changes. Only a few open activities need no registration.
**TOM:** How do I sign up for an activity?
**ANNA:** You contact the club that organizes it: by phone, by email, or through its website. It depends on the club.
**TOM:** Here is a question that matters for many of our listeners. What if I don't speak Luxembourgish?
**ANNA:** In general, that is not a problem. Luxembourgish, French and German are the most common languages in the clubs, and it depends on the activity and on the trainer.
**TOM:** So the best idea is to ask the club when I register.
**ANNA:** Yes, exactly. And an activity like a walk, a dance class or a shared lunch is also a friendly way to practise a language.
**TOM:** And when do the activities take place? I imagine it is mostly during the day.
**ANNA:** Many are during the day, but some take place in the evening, and sometimes at the weekend. The programme of each club gives the details.
**TOM:** What about people who have difficulty walking, or who use a wheelchair?
**ANNA:** The advice of the clubs is simple: contact the club before you register, and ask about access for the activity you have chosen.
**TOM:** You said earlier that the clubs also give advice. What kind of advice is that?
**ANNA:** Each club has a professional team. They can talk with you, in confidence, about the move into retirement, about ageing in good health, or about housing and care services for older people.
**TOM:** And what if they don't have the answer themselves?
**ANNA:** Then they point you to the right service. It is a good first door to knock on.
**TOM:** Can people also give something back, and not only take part?
**ANNA:** Yes, and the clubs encourage it. You can become a volunteer, and you can even suggest a new activity that you would like to lead or to share.
**TOM:** So someone who knows how to bake, to paint or to repair a bicycle could share that skill.
**ANNA:** Exactly. Passing on knowledge between generations and between cultures is part of the clubs' charter.
**TOM:** Now let's look at the website. What do we find on clubaktivplus dot l-u?
**ANNA:** The website exists in French and in German. Its most useful page is the list of all the clubs.
**TOM:** And what does the page of a club show?
**ANNA:** It shows the address, the phone number, the opening hours and the partner communes. And you can download the current activity programme of that club.
**TOM:** Is there one big calendar with the activities of all the clubs together?
**ANNA:** No, each club publishes its own programme, several times a year. But there is a nice shortcut, and it is called the ClubPages.
**TOM:** And what exactly are these ClubPages?
**ANNA:** It is a small brochure that comes out four times a year. It presents a selection of activities from all the clubs, so you get a taste of everything.
**TOM:** And what else can we find on the website?
**ANNA:** There is a page with frequently asked questions, which is very clear. There are news and events, and also job offers for people who would like to work in a club.
**TOM:** And what if I still have a question after all that?
**ANNA:** Then you can write to the national coordination of the clubs. The address is contact at clubaktivplus dot l-u.
**TOM:** Let me ask one last question. Why do these clubs matter so much?
**ANNA:** Because being alone too much is not good for anyone. The clubs want to prevent loneliness, and to help people stay active and stay part of society.
**TOM:** And for someone who is new in Luxembourg, it is also a simple way to meet people from the region.
**ANNA:** Yes. You share an activity, you talk, and little by little, you know people.
**TOM:** Let's bring it together. What should people remember?
**ANNA:** First: the Clubs Aktiv Plus are meeting places for people who want to stay active around retirement age, with no age limit.
**TOM:** Second: there are more than twenty of them across the country, and you are welcome in every club, wherever you live.
**ANNA:** Third: the choice is wide, from sport and day trips to language and computer courses. Most activities have a price, and some are free.
**TOM:** And fourth: to take part, you look at the programme of a club and you contact that club directly.
**ANNA:** You can find all the clubs and their programmes on clubaktivplus dot l-u.
**TOM:** Stay active, stay curious, and stay in good company.
**ANNA:** And why not try one activity this month? That was our episode about the Clubs Aktiv Plus. This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt, a programme that favours living together in Luxembourg: discovering the country, and meeting new people. If you want to discover more activities offered by the Biergerpakt, such as guided visits, online information sessions, a language learning mobile application, creative workshops or training sessions, simply head over to biergerpakt.lu where you will find detailed information. It is open to all residents, luxembourgish and non-luxembourgish, as well as to cross-border workers. And the activities are free of charge. You simply need to join the Biergerpakt for free at biergerpakt.lu.
**TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care, and see you next time!
**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
