# Podcast Script — "Social Security – How You Are Insured in Luxembourg"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 11 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** I have a question to start with, Anna. Do you remember your very first payslip in Luxembourg?
**ANNA:** Oh yes, I remember the surprise: the amount at the top was clearly bigger than the amount that arrived on my bank account.
**TOM:** And did you wonder where the difference went?
**ANNA:** I did. A part of it is tax, and another part is social security contributions. Today we look at that second part: social security in Luxembourg, and how you become a member of it.
**TOM:** And one organisation plays a central role here: the Social Security Centre, known by the four letters CCSS.
**ANNA:** The letters come from its French name. Its website is ccss dot l-u, and it exists in French, German and English.
**TOM:** Before we talk about the Centre, let's make sure we understand the big picture. What does social security actually cover?
**ANNA:** It protects you in the big moments of life: when you are ill or expecting a baby, when you have an accident at work, when you need long-term care, and when you retire.
**TOM:** So those are four different insurances?
**ANNA:** Yes, there are four branches: health and maternity insurance, accident insurance, long-term care insurance, and pension insurance.
**TOM:** And each branch has its own institution, if I understand correctly.
**ANNA:** That's right. For most employees, health is handled by the National Health Fund, the CNS. Pensions are handled by the National Pension Insurance Fund, and accidents at work by the Accident Insurance Association.
**TOM:** We have separate episodes about several of them, by the way. So where does the Social Security Centre fit in?
**ANNA:** You can think of it as the front door for all of them. The Centre registers the insured people, it calculates the contributions, and it collects them. Then the money goes to the different institutions.
**TOM:** So the Centre does not pay my doctor's bill or my pension.
**ANNA:** No, it doesn't. The health fund reimburses your doctor's bill, and the pension fund pays your pension. The Centre makes sure that you are registered and that the contributions are paid.
**TOM:** Let's talk about that registration. The official word is "affiliation". Who has to be affiliated?
**ANNA:** Everyone who works in Luxembourg for pay, as an employee or as a self-employed person. It is not a choice: it is compulsory.
**TOM:** Does that include cross-border workers, who live in France, Belgium or Germany?
**ANNA:** Yes. As a rule, if you work in Luxembourg, you are insured in Luxembourg, wherever you live. If you also work in another country, the rules can be different, and it is best to ask the Centre.
**TOM:** Imagine I have just signed my first employment contract here. What do I have to do to be affiliated?
**ANNA:** You don't have to do anything, because your employer handles all the formalities. They send a declaration of entry to the Centre within eight days of your first day at work.
**TOM:** And what if I have never had a Luxembourg social security number?
**ANNA:** Then your employer adds a copy of your identity document, and the Centre creates your number. It has thirteen digits, and people often call it the "matricule".
**TOM:** What do I receive after that?
**ANNA:** Two things arrive by post. First, a letter that confirms your affiliation. And then your social security card, usually within about three weeks.
**TOM:** Let's look at that card for a moment. What is it for?
**ANNA:** You show it to every healthcare provider, for example your doctor, the hospital or the pharmacy. It carries your name and your thirteen-digit number.
**TOM:** And I heard that the card has two sides.
**ANNA:** It does. One side is the national side, for Luxembourg. The other side is the European Health Insurance Card. It helps you to get urgent care during a temporary stay in the European Union and in a few neighbouring countries, such as Switzerland and Norway.
**TOM:** Does the European side stay valid forever?
**ANNA:** No, it has an expiry date. But a new card normally arrives automatically, about two months before that date.
**TOM:** And what should I do if I lose my card?
**ANNA:** You order a new one on the Centre's website, and it is free. And if you need a proof urgently, your health fund can give you a provisional certificate through MyGuichet dot l-u.
**TOM:** Now the contributions. How does the money get from my salary to the social security?
**ANNA:** Every month, your employer tells the Centre how much you earned and how many hours you worked. The Centre calculates the contributions and sends the bill to your employer.
**TOM:** So does my employer pay everything?
**ANNA:** Your employer pays two parts: their own share, and your share, which they take from your gross salary. That is the line you see on your payslip.
**TOM:** So I pay a part, and my employer pays a part on top.
**ANNA:** Exactly. It is a system of solidarity: everyone who works contributes, and everyone is protected when they need it.
**TOM:** Is there anything I should check myself?
**ANNA:** Yes, one document in particular. Once a year, the Centre sends you a statement of your pension insurance record. It lists the income that was registered for you in Luxembourg.
**TOM:** And why is that statement so important?
**ANNA:** Because your future pension is calculated from this record. If something is missing or wrong, tell the Centre as soon as possible, and keep your payslips as proof.
**TOM:** What about my family? Are my husband or wife and my children covered too?
**ANNA:** They can be. A spouse or partner and your children can be co-insured with you for health care, under certain conditions. For this, you contact your health fund, and not the Centre.
**TOM:** And what happens when a baby is born?
**ANNA:** For newborn babies, co-insurance is automatic, and the baby's card is sent to you without a request.
**TOM:** Let's take other situations. What if I am self-employed?
**ANNA:** Then there is no employer to do it for you. You register yourself with the Centre when you start your activity, and you pay your contributions yourself.
**TOM:** And what about a student who works during the summer holidays?
**ANNA:** The employer declares the student, just like any employee. But a holiday job is a special case: through this job, the student is insured only against accidents at work.
**TOM:** Here is a situation many families know. I would like to hire someone to clean my home, or to look after my children. Do I have to declare that person?
**ANNA:** Yes, you do. The moment you pay someone to work in your home, you are an employer, and that person has the right to be insured.
**TOM:** That sounds like a lot of paperwork for a private person.
**ANNA:** It is much simpler than people think. There is a simplified procedure for private households. You tell the Centre the net hourly wage that you pay, and the Centre calculates everything else: the gross salary, the contributions and the tax.
**TOM:** And can I do that online?
**ANNA:** Yes, on MyGuichet dot l-u. And the result matters: the person who works for you is covered in case of illness or an accident, and they build up a pension.
**TOM:** Now a less happy moment: my job ends. What happens to my social security?
**ANNA:** Your employer informs the Centre, and you receive a letter saying that your affiliation has ended.
**TOM:** Does that mean I lose my health cover from one day to the next?
**ANNA:** Not immediately, in most cases. If you live in Luxembourg and you were insured without a break for at least six months, your health cover continues for the rest of the month and for three more months. Cross-border workers ask the health fund of the country where they live.
**TOM:** And what are my options after those three months?
**ANNA:** There are several possibilities. A new job brings a new affiliation. You may be co-insured through your spouse or partner. And if neither applies, you can ask the Centre for voluntary health insurance.
**TOM:** With voluntary insurance, I suppose I pay the contributions myself.
**ANNA:** Yes. And the timing is important: if you apply within three months after your compulsory insurance ended, your insurance simply continues. If you apply later, no costs are reimbursed during the first three months.
**TOM:** Is there something similar for the pension?
**ANNA:** There is. People who no longer contribute through a job can join the voluntary pension insurance, so that their pension record continues.
**TOM:** And how do I know what that would cost me?
**ANNA:** The Centre's website has an online simulator for voluntary pension insurance. It shows you your options before you decide.
**TOM:** Let's stay with the online services. What can I do without going anywhere?
**ANNA:** Quite a lot. On the website ccss dot l-u, you find explanations for employees, employers, self-employed people and farmers, and all the forms.
**TOM:** And what does MyGuichet add to that?
**ANNA:** In your private space on MyGuichet dot l-u, you can create a certificate of affiliation in a few moments and download it. It lists the jobs that are registered for you.
**TOM:** How can somebody else be sure that such a certificate is genuine?
**ANNA:** The certificate carries a QR code. With the free GouvCheck app, anyone can scan the code and check that the document is real.
**TOM:** Can I also receive my documents from the Centre online?
**ANNA:** Yes, if you switch on electronic delivery for the Centre in MyGuichet. Then the documents arrive in your private space instead of your letterbox.
**TOM:** And what if I prefer to speak to a person?
**ANNA:** You can call the Centre or use the contact form. The counters are in Luxembourg City, but only by appointment, and you can book the appointment online.
**TOM:** What happens when I move house? Do I have to tell the Centre?
**ANNA:** If you live in Luxembourg, you declare your new address at your commune, and the Centre is informed automatically. Cross-border workers can report their new address through MyGuichet dot l-u.
**TOM:** One last point, and it is an important one: fake messages.
**ANNA:** Yes. Criminals send emails and text messages that look as if they come from the Centre. Often they claim that your social security card has expired.
**TOM:** And what are they trying to get from me?
**ANNA:** Your bank details or your LuxTrust codes. The Centre never asks for these to renew a card, and the card never costs anything. So don't click on the link, and go to the official website yourself.
**TOM:** Let's bring it together. What should people remember?
**ANNA:** First: if you work in Luxembourg, you are insured for health, accidents, long-term care and pension. Your employer registers you with the Social Security Centre, the CCSS.
**TOM:** Second: you receive a thirteen-digit number and a social security card. Keep the card with you, also when you travel in Europe.
**ANNA:** Third: check your yearly pension statement, and report mistakes quickly.
**TOM:** Fourth: if you employ someone at home, declare them. The simplified procedure makes it easy.
**ANNA:** And fifth: when a job ends, your health cover does not stop the same day, but act within three months if you need voluntary insurance.
**TOM:** You can find all the information on ccss dot l-u.
**ANNA:** Social security works because everyone takes part. That was our episode about social security and the Social Security Centre.
