# Podcast Script — "Lëtz prepare – Ready for 72 Hours"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 9 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.
**TOM:** Hello everyone!
**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.
**TOM:** Anna, a question to start. Imagine the electricity goes off tonight, in the whole region, and it stays off for three days. Would you be ready?
**ANNA:** Me? I have a few candles somewhere, and half a packet of biscuits.
**TOM:** Then today's episode is for you, and for me too. We're talking about Lëtz prepare.
**ANNA:** Lëtz prepare is the name of Luxembourg's National Resilience Strategy. It's a play on words: "Lëtz", like Lëtzebuerg, and the English "let's prepare".
**TOM:** The website is letzprepare dot l-u. And its motto is: "Together, for our protection."
**ANNA:** First, that big word, resilience. What does it mean?
**TOM:** Resilience is the ability to resist a shock, to adapt, and to get back to normal quickly. That's true for a country, and also for each one of us.
**ANNA:** And what kind of shocks are we talking about?
**TOM:** The strategy looks at all kinds of risks together. Extreme weather and floods, a long power cut, cyberattacks, a pandemic, an industrial or nuclear accident, a terrorist threat, and even armed conflict.
**ANNA:** That sounds a bit frightening.
**TOM:** The idea is not to frighten anyone. It's the opposite. When you are prepared, you stay calmer, and you can help others.
**ANNA:** Who is behind the strategy?
**TOM:** The government. The work was coordinated by the High Commission for National Protection, together with a group of ministries. It is Luxembourg's first National Resilience Strategy, and it was launched in 2025.
**ANNA:** And it's built on eight pillars, right?
**TOM:** Yes. They go from protecting democracy and the institutions of the State, to essential services like water, energy, health and transport, and on to a strong economy, cyber security, civil defence and military defence.
**ANNA:** And one of these pillars is about us. It's called "a resilient society".
**TOM:** Exactly. Because in a big crisis, the emergency services cannot be everywhere at the same time. They may not be able to help everyone immediately.
**ANNA:** So if most households can manage on their own for a few days, the helpers can concentrate on the people who need them most.
**TOM:** That's why, in October 2026, the government launched a national campaign with a question in Luxembourgish: "Bass du prett?"
**ANNA:** That means: "Are you ready?"
**TOM:** And with the campaign came a practical booklet: "My guide to 72 hours of self-sufficiency".
**ANNA:** Why 72 hours?
**TOM:** 72 hours is three days. The idea is that every household should be able to manage on its own during the first three days of a crisis.
**ANNA:** The guide has four parts. Understand the risks. Know how to react. Prepare with a checklist. And write a personal emergency plan.
**TOM:** Let's start with how to react. How do I even know that something serious is happening?
**ANNA:** Through LU-Alert, the national warning system. A message arrives directly on your mobile phone. There are also the LU-Alert app and website, the media, and the sirens.
**TOM:** We have a whole episode about LU-Alert, by the way.
**ANNA:** The warnings use colours. Yellow means: be aware. Orange means: be careful. Red means: utmost vigilance. And purple means imminent danger: act immediately.
**TOM:** And the radio is very important. When the power is off and the internet doesn't work, a radio with batteries still does.
**ANNA:** Or a wind-up radio. Or simply the radio in your car.
**TOM:** The emergency numbers stay the same: 112 for the fire and rescue services, and 1-1-3 for the police.
**ANNA:** But only for a real emergency. In a crisis, keep your phone calls short, so the lines stay free for the emergency services.
**TOM:** And one more rule: check information before you share it. Trust the official sources, like infocrise dot l-u, and don't pass on rumours.
**ANNA:** Now the checklist. What should I have at home?
**TOM:** First, water. The guide says: at least three litres per person, per day.
**ANNA:** So for three days, that's nine litres for each person. For a family of four, thirty-six litres.
**TOM:** Then food that keeps for a long time and is easy to eat. Tins, biscuits, nuts, energy bars. And don't forget a tin opener!
**ANNA:** And food for your pets, if you have any.
**TOM:** A small gas stove can help too. But be careful: never use a barbecue or a generator inside the house. The fumes can kill.
**ANNA:** Next, light and warmth. A torch with spare batteries, candles with matches, and blankets or sleeping bags.
**TOM:** For your health: a first aid kit, and the medicines you take regularly.
**ANNA:** Hygiene products too: toilet paper, wet wipes and disinfectant gel.
**TOM:** Then information and energy: the radio we mentioned, and a power bank to charge your phone.
**ANNA:** And something people often forget: cash. If the power is off, paying by card may not work.
**TOM:** Good point. And keep copies of your important documents, like identity papers and insurance contracts, in a waterproof pouch.
**ANNA:** That's a long list. Do I have to buy everything tomorrow?
**TOM:** No. You probably have many of these things at home already. The checklist helps you see what is missing.
**ANNA:** And the guide says: check your kit at least twice a year, for example the expiry dates.
**TOM:** Now the fourth part, the personal emergency plan. What is that?
**ANNA:** It's a form that you fill in together with the people you live with, and that you share with family and friends.
**TOM:** What goes into it?
**ANNA:** Who lives in the home, with phone numbers, and medical information like allergies and regular medication.
**TOM:** And practical things about the home. Where is the main water valve? Where is the circuit breaker? Where is the fire extinguisher? How do you open the garage door without electricity?
**ANNA:** I have no idea how to open my garage without electricity!
**TOM:** Then it's good to find out now, and not in the dark.
**ANNA:** The plan also asks: where do we meet if we cannot go home and the phones don't work? You write down two meeting places.
**TOM:** And who collects the children from school if the parents can't? How do I get home from work without a car or public transport?
**ANNA:** There's also a part about the neighbourhood. Who could help me? And who might need my help?
**TOM:** Maybe a neighbour knows first aid. And maybe an older neighbour lives alone and would be glad if someone knocked on the door.
**ANNA:** That's very Biergerpakt, actually. When we know our neighbours, everyone is stronger.
**TOM:** And keep the plan on paper too, not only on your phone. A battery can run out.
**ANNA:** Where can people find all this?
**TOM:** On letzprepare dot l-u. You can download the guide, the checklist and the emergency plan for free.
**ANNA:** They exist in five languages: Luxembourgish, French, German, English and Portuguese.
**TOM:** And for advice on specific situations, like a power cut, a flood or a nuclear alert, there's the website infocrise dot l-u.
**ANNA:** Let's bring it together. What should people remember?
**TOM:** First: Lëtz prepare is Luxembourg's National Resilience Strategy. Being prepared is a task for the State, and for all of us.
**ANNA:** Second: every household should be able to manage on its own for 72 hours.
**TOM:** Third: think of water, food, light, a radio, your medicines, some cash, and copies of your documents.
**ANNA:** And fourth: fill in your personal emergency plan together with your family, and talk to your neighbours.
**TOM:** A little preparation today makes a big difference tomorrow.
**ANNA:** And together, we are stronger. That was our episode about Lëtz prepare. **ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
