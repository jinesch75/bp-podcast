# Podcast Script — "Clarvia: Guiding Families Through What Comes Next"

**Part of the Biergerpakt programme**
**Hosts:** Anna (woman) and Tom (man)
**Length:** about 10 minutes — spoken slowly, in simple English

---

**ANNA:** Hello, and welcome! It's really nice to have you with us today.

**TOM:** Hello everyone!

**ANNA:** This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt. That's a programme of living together in Luxembourg, organized by the Ministry of Family Affairs, Solidarity, Living Together and Reception of Refugees. The idea is to help residents, and also cross-border workers, to discover Luxembourg — or maybe re-discover Luxembourg — and to meet new people.

**TOM:** Exactly. And today we talk about a topic that is not easy... but that touches every family at some point. What happens, on the practical side, when we lose someone we love. There is a new Luxembourg initiative that wants to help with exactly that. It's called Clarvia.

**ANNA:** Clarvia. And you're right, Tom, it's a difficult topic. But maybe that's exactly why we should talk about it.

**TOM:** That's the idea. Because when someone passes away, the family is suddenly expected to handle a lot of things. Administration. Documents. Institutions to notify. Funeral matters. Questions about the succession. And all of this... while they are in shock, and grieving.

**ANNA:** And some things have to happen very quickly, right?

**TOM:** Yes. In Luxembourg, a death must be declared to the commune within twenty-four hours. So some first formalities come at the very moment when families have the least energy to deal with them.

**ANNA:** Twenty-four hours... that's very little time. And how many people does this touch, here in Luxembourg?

**TOM:** More than you might think. There were about four thousand five hundred deaths in Luxembourg in 2024. And research estimates that for each death, around nine people close to that person are affected. So we are talking about more than forty thousand people, every year, just in Luxembourg.

**ANNA:** Forty thousand people a year. And for many of them, I imagine, it's even harder — because of language.

**TOM:** Exactly. Almost half of Luxembourg's residents — around forty-seven percent — are foreign nationals. Many families are spread across different countries. Some don't speak French or German. The information usually exists somewhere, on official websites... but it's scattered, it's technical, and it's hard to find at the worst possible moment.

**ANNA:** So that's the problem. Now tell us — what is Clarvia?

**TOM:** Clarvia is a Luxembourg non-profit — an ASBL — founded in 2026. Their goal is to build a free, multilingual bereavement service. In simple words: a service that guides families through the practical steps after a loss. What needs to be done. What is urgent. Which documents matter. And where to find qualified help.

**ANNA:** A guide for the practical side of loss. And how does it work, concretely?

**TOM:** The heart of it is a digital checklist. You answer a few questions about your situation, and the service gives you a personalised, step-by-step list of the administrative things to take care of. An early version — they call it an alpha version — is already available on their website, clarvia.org.

**ANNA:** Personalised — so not the same list for everybody?

**TOM:** Right. Because every situation is different. Was the person working, or retired? Is the family in Luxembourg, or partly abroad? The relevant steps change. And the checklist also helps you see which steps are time-sensitive — the things with a deadline — so you know what really cannot wait.

**ANNA:** And what kind of topics does it cover?

**TOM:** The practical world around a loss. The first administrative steps. The documents to collect and keep. The institutions and organisations to notify. Funeral-related administration. Then things like the employer, the pension, insurance, banking, housing... and a first orientation on succession questions. Plus links to the official sources, and to qualified professionals.

**ANNA:** That's really the whole journey. Now, one thing I want to check — is this reliable? Where does the information come from?

**TOM:** Very important point. Every step in the checklist is mapped back to an official government source — for example Guichet.lu. Clarvia keeps a public registry of these sources, so families and professionals can verify where each recommendation comes from. It's not somebody's opinion — it's official information, made easier to follow.

**ANNA:** Good. And I have to ask the question we ask in every episode... what about languages?

**TOM:** It's multilingual by design. The website exists in English, French, German and Luxembourgish. Because in a country where nearly half the residents are foreign nationals, grief support simply has to speak several languages.

**ANNA:** Absolutely. And what does it cost?

**TOM:** Nothing. It's free, for every family. No fees, no advertising, no premium version. Clarvia is a non-profit — it's supported by sponsors, grants and volunteers. And one more thing that matters: the checklist is designed to protect your privacy. It does not collect, store or share your personal data.

**ANNA:** That's reassuring. What about families with connections to other countries? You said many families are spread out.

**TOM:** Yes — and that's a special focus. Clarvia starts with Luxembourg, but it also looks at the cross-border situations — with France, Germany, Belgium and Portugal — for cross-border workers and migrant families who may face obligations in more than one country. Losing someone is hard enough. When two countries' rules are involved, a clear guide helps even more.

**ANNA:** Now, Tom, let's be clear about the limits too. What is Clarvia not?

**TOM:** Good question. Clarvia gives practical guidance and orientation — it does not replace professional advice. For legal, notarial, tax, banking or succession decisions, you still need the qualified professionals. Clarvia helps you understand where to start, what to prepare, and when to seek that professional support. And of course, it is not an emergency service — in an emergency, you call one-one-two.

**ANNA:** So it's the map, not the whole journey. Where does the project stand today?

**TOM:** It's young. Clarvia is in its build and validation phase — the full service is not launched yet. But the first version of the checklist is already public, and the project is being shaped together with families, caregivers and professionals. Everything they build is open — you can even follow their progress online.

**ANNA:** And can our listeners help?

**TOM:** They can, in a very human way. If you have already managed the practical steps after losing someone in Luxembourg, you can share your experience on clarvia.org — what was hardest, which documents were difficult, what would have helped. You don't need to share private details. That real-life experience helps them build a better service. And they also welcome volunteers, translators and professionals who work with bereaved families.

**ANNA:** That's a beautiful way to turn a hard experience into help for others. Okay, Tom — a short summary?

**TOM:** Of course. Clarvia is a Luxembourg non-profit building a free, multilingual guide for families after the loss of a loved one. A personalised checklist shows you the administrative steps, the urgent deadlines, the documents, and the institutions — always based on official sources — in English, French, German and Luxembourgish. It's free, it protects your privacy, and the first version is already on clarvia.org.

**ANNA:** And the message for today... if you are going through a loss, you don't have to figure out the practical side alone. And if you have been through it, your experience can help the next family.

**TOM:** Exactly. Nobody should have to navigate grief and paperwork at the same time, without help.

**ANNA:** That was our episode about Clarvia. This podcast is part of the Biergerpakt, a programme that favours living together in Luxembourg: discovering the country, and meeting new people. If you want to discover more activities offered by the Biergerpakt, such as guided visits, online information sessions, a language learning mobile application, creative workshops or training sessions, simply head over to biergerpakt.lu where you will find detailed information. It is open to all residents, luxembourgish and non-luxembourgish, as well as to cross-border workers. And the activities are free of charge. You simply need to join the Biergerpakt for free at biergerpakt.lu.

**TOM:** Thank you, everyone. Take care... and see you next time!

**ANNA:** Thank you so much for listening.
